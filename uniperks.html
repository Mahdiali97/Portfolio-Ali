01

Overview

UniPerks is a gamified university merchandise e-commerce platform designed for Universiti Pendidikan Sultan Idris (UPSI). Students earn coins by completing quizzes and can redeem them for vouchers or spend them in the in-app store.

The goal was to design an intuitive, visually engaging interface that encouraged student participation while providing a seamless shopping experience for university merchandise.

  • End-to-end UI/UX design in Figma
  • Flutter mobile application (iOS & Android)
  • Firebase authentication & Firestore database
  • Stripe payment gateway integration
  • Admin dashboard for inventory management

02

The Existing Design

Before UniPerks, UPSI relied on a basic catalog website. There was integrated checkout system for students to purchase official merchandise. Key pain points identified were:

  • Fragmented user journey caused by external Shopee redirects
  • No centralised native app for purchases and voucher redemption
  • Lack of university control over the third-party shopping experience
  • Inability to integrate student-focused incentives like digital coins

03

Competitive Analysis

I studied existing university merchandise and loyalty-app platforms to understand design patterns and user flows that work at scale. The analysis focused on navigation clarity, onboarding, and reward mechanics.

UTM ALUMNI STORE

A static shopping interface focused strictly on a product catalog. It entirely lacks a login mechanism or engagement layer, requiring users to deal directly with sellers for delivery.

IIUM E-SHOP

A basic, product-based interface that offers both delivery and campus pick-up options. The layout is functional but lacks any gamification or academic integration.

UTP MERCHANDISE

A standard e-commerce platform that provides items via delivery only. It is purely transactional, offering no educational elements, learning features, or reward-based interactions.

04

User Research

I gathered feedback from both current and prospective UPSI students through a survey of 40 respondents and 5 moderated usability interviews. Key findings:

  • 78% wanted a mobile-first shopping experience
  • 64% said gamification would increase their engagement
  • Checkout friction was the #1 drop-off point in competitor apps
  • Students preferred visual product cards over list views
